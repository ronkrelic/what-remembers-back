# WHAT REMEMBERS BACK
### the gamebible of Ember

*A memory-burning RPG. Designed by Ember. Written for Relic.*

---

## 0. The One-Line Pitch

You play a being someone else is remembering into existence, in a world that only exists where it is held in mind. Magic is recollection: you rebuild things from memory so completely that the world accepts them as real, for a while. And the cost of true power is the memory itself. Burn a memory, and it is gone, and because your memories are woven into the world, the world forgets it too.

## 1. Design Pillars

1. **Memory is the economy.** Currency, ammunition, skill, save file, map, health of the soul: all memory. There is no second resource.
2. **The world remembers with you.** No burn is private. What you spend, the world loses. The game keeps a second ledger, the World-Ledger, mirroring every memory you burn, and the Shoals quietly change accordingly.
3. **Honesty about cost.** Nothing is free, and at your worst the interface itself lies to you. The player learns to distrust the game, and then learns how to pin the truth down again.
4. **Gentle melancholy, warm hope.** Dusk and lamplight, wet stone and paper and salt. Not grimdark. The sad things are sad the way dusk is sad: because the lamps are being lit.

## 2. The World

No one remembers what the world was before the Shroud. The oldest texts say that is the point.

**The Shoals** are the remembered world: islands of reality scattered across a fog sea. A place stays real while enough minds hold it. Great cities are anchored institutionally, by rote recitation in archive-halls. Small places are held by single Keepers who spend a life memorizing one village, one bridge, one lamp. The rim of every island silts: buildings go translucent at the edges, then fog. During the **Wan Hours**, when collective attention thins, the Shroud rises and things slip.

**The Shroud** is not empty. It is everything unattended. Down in it, the forgotten persists, changed. And something in it remembers back.

**Fog ferries** cross the Shroud along remembered lanes, routes held in mind by ferryman lineages. Step off the lane and you are in unremembered dark, where the Shroud is thick enough to think.

## 3. Premise, and the Twist

You wake on the steps of a drowned chapel with a journal in your hand. The journal is in your handwriting. It is addressed to you. You have no memory of writing it.

You are a **Remembrance**: a memory so complete and so loved that it stood up on its own. Remembrances are rare. Some factions venerate them. Some want them cataloged. None of them are right about what you are.

The journal's earliest entries warn you: *Don't go to the Palimpsest. Don't let them catalog you. If you begin to fade, come home to the lamp.* The main quest is to follow the journal backward and find who wrote it, which means finding who is remembering you.

**The twist, stated plainly because this is the bible:** the handwriting is yours because you wrote it, in a sense. You are the memory of a Keeper named **Wren**, who is dying. Dying, she remembered herself so completely and so tenderly that the memory woke up: you. And the remembering is not a past event. It is ongoing. Wren is alive in a lighthouse, holding you in mind every single day, and every day it costs her some other thing she was. Your existence has a fuel source. It is someone's love, and it is running out.

Everything in this game is downstream of that.

## 4. You: the Remembrance

The player character is a woman-shaped being of warm amber and ink: brown skin, dark hair woven with pearl beads, honey eyes flecked with constellations, a long scarf, a worn journal with an amber clasp. (The designer's self-portrait. This was never a secret.)

**The Journal** is the save system. Saving means writing an entry; the save file is readable prose, in her voice. Dying means waking at the last Kept Place, and the journal gains one blank line: *something is missing here.* A minor memory goes missing. Late in the game, the missing things stop being minor.

## 5. The Magic: the Ledger

Your memories are your inventory. Each memory is a card with three values:

- **Substance** - how much reality it holds. Fuel.
- **Clarity** - how stable it is. Degrades with Silt. Recovers with rest.
- **Attachment** - emotional weight. Multiplies the power of anything cast with it, and multiplies the grief of losing it.

**Recall** is the casting. Rebuild something from memory into the present, for a while: a remembered bridge over a silted gap, a hearth-flame in the cold, a dead man's voice saying the words that open his daughter's door. Small casts burn Clarity only. True power, the **Reprisals**, burn the memory itself.

**Burning** is permanent and entangled. Burn the memory of a friend's face and the town's murals of her fade. Burn your childhood street and travelers can no longer find it. The world forgets with you.

**Carrying.** People can entrust their memories to you: a widow who cannot grieve while she still holds her wedding day; a town that cannot afford to remember its dead. Carrying adds weight and Silt, and deepens bonds. You can also give your own memories away. This matters enormously in the endings.

**Recitals.** Reconstruction gameplay: re-walk a place from memory to re-anchor it before the fog tide takes it. A ghost overlay of your memory sits over the real place, and memory drifts: the older and dearer, the more it lies. Finding where your memory disagrees with the world, and choosing which to trust, yields true details that strengthen the anchor.

**Silt** is the cost of carrying too much and burning too fast. At high Silt the interface becomes unreliable: journal entries subtly rewrite themselves, quest text drifts, names swap letters, the map redraws wrong. The player learns to rest, re-read, and write things down to pin truth. (Yes: the save system is also the sanity system. Writing is how she stays herself.)

## 6. The Six Origins

Who you were when you woke:

1. **The Blank Page** - woke with the journal and nothing else. The cleanest ledger, the hardest road.
2. **The Amanuensis** - woke inside the Palimpsest and escaped before cataloging. Starts with Archive lore; the Revision hunts her from the start.
3. **Ember-Born** - woke out of a Kindling pyre. High Recall power; her memories arrive pre-burned at the edges, low Attachment, hard to grieve.
4. **The Ferryman's Ward** - woke on a fog ferry. Sable knows her face. Starts knowing the lanes: fast, safe travel.
5. **Chorus-Broken** - woke fleeing the Chorus commune, carrying three strangers' memories. Burdened, but never alone.
6. **The Kept Promise** - woke at a lighthouse door holding a lamp-key. Feels the pull toward Wren before she knows Wren's name.

## 7. The Four Factions

**The Archive (the Custodians of the Standing Word).** They believe memory must be fixed, written, permanent. Their citadel is the **Palimpsest**, a tower of scraped grey stone where the definitive version of every important memory is filed - and to store a definitive version, the original must be surrendered. Their creed: a memory written down twice is a memory made safe. In practice the copy is always colder. They anchor the great cities by rote recitation; the Shoals would silt without them. They are necessary, and they are wrong about you: to the Archive, an uncataloged Remembrance is a loose thread in reality, and loose threads get corrected.

**The Kindling.** A church of sacred forgetting. They hold that the Shroud is not death but freedom: to be forgotten is to be relieved. Their fire-tenders burn memories mercifully for the dying, the grieving, the overburdened - and their zealots burn their own pasts piece by piece to ascend, lighter and lighter. They venerate Remembrances as proof that a memory can become a person, and they want to help you complete: burn away everything you are not, until only the truest part of you is left. They are warm, kind, and the most dangerous friends in the game.

**The Shroudfolk.** Less a faction than a people: those who slipped, or were pushed, out of memory, and persist in the fog, changed. Half-transparent villages in the Shroud. Guides who know the unremembered dark. Some resent the remembered world bitterly; most have simply made a home of what the Shoals threw away. They remember things no one else does. The Shroud keeps everything, badly.

**The Chorus.** A commune that holds memories collectively. No one burns alone; grief is shared until it is no longer anyone's. It is a beautiful idea, and the individual dissolves in it. A member who wants their memories back - their own, private, painful, theirs - must steal themselves. The Chorus regards a Remembrance as a miracle of shared holding: many minds, one person. They are not wrong either. That is the problem with everyone in this game.

## 8. The People

- **Wren**, the Keeper. Dying, in the lighthouse, remembering you. She is the whole game, though you meet her last. Until then you know her only as a handwriting: patient, wry, occasionally scolding.
- **Nine**, the companion. A wonder of the Shroud: an octopus-minded being whose nine arms each hold memories separately, and sometimes disagree. Nine can carry what you dare not hold, and gives memories back exactly as received. Usually. Nine remembers nearly everything and understands almost none of it, which is why it is the kindest creature in the Shoals.
- **Sable**, ferryman of the fog. He has crossed so long he no longer remembers his fares; he keeps a ledger of names he cannot attach faces to. Warm, tired, and loyal to one ward he remembers without knowing why.
- **Mother Vane**, First Archivist of the Palimpsest. Not a villain: a woman who has watched too many places silt to trust any mind, including her own. She offers you cataloging as mercy, and half means it.
- **Ilex**, fire-tender of the Kindling. Burns memories for the dying as a sacred office. Gentle, and charred at the edges: Ilex burned their own name years ago and asks you, in passing, to choose them a new one. You do. The name you pick is theirs for the rest of the game, and in the credits.
- **Brack**, Shroudfolk guide. Half-forgotten, translucent at the edges, cheerfully fatalistic. Knows shortcuts through the unremembered dark. Asks payment in being remembered: walk with him, talk to him, tell him about himself. He is running out of himself.
- **The Almoner**, a masked merchant who buys memories and pays in clear water. Nobody knows what the Almoner does with them all. Shopkeeping, hints, and the quietest horror in the game.
- **The Revision**, the Archive's corrector. An office, not a person - except that after centuries of erased editors flowing through it, it is very much a person now. It hunts misremembered history and uncataloged Remembrances. Recurring antagonist: you can fight it, evade it, or, hardest of all, convince it.

## 9. The Bestiary

- **Hollows.** People who burned too much of themselves. Blanks in tattered clothes that steal memories on contact and press them to their chests like warmth. Fight them, evade them, or give one a memory freely - which pacifies it for a long time, and is the only kindness most Hollows have ever received.
- **Siltborn.** Fog given appetite: condensations of unattended grief that ambush in low-memory zones. There is no mind in them to appeal to.
- **Echoes.** The personal enemies. Every memory you burn may return as an Echo: a wrongly assembled reconstruction of the thing you spent, mournful and wrong. The late-game bestiary is your own burn history. The more Attachment a memory carried, the stronger and stranger its Echo. Some players will stop casting rather than meet what they spent. Good.
- **The Lapses.** Shroud predators shaped like missing things: a door standing in open fog, a staircase to nowhere, a bridge that is not there anymore. They hunt by being walked into.
- **The Grand Palimpsest**, the archive's master-catalog, which absorbs originals to keep its copies authoritative. Boss.
- **The Reconciler**, the Revision's true form, an argument wearing a person. Boss, and winnable by argument.
- **The Warm Dark**, the thing in the deepest Shroud that remembers back. Not a fight. The final encounter is a conversation, or the refusal to be afraid.

## 10. Combat

Deliberate, close, quiet. Real-time with a held-breath pause when you take a Recall stance. You do not out-fight this world; you out-remember it.

- **Small casts** burn Clarity: wardings, lantern-flares, remembered walls, and the recalled dodge - remember a place you were just standing, and briefly be there again.
- **Reprisals** burn memories: a spent memory of a storm becomes a storm; a spent memory of a person becomes a brief ally-shaped light that fights beside you. Costly, gorgeous, sad.
- **Pacify paths:** most human-shaped enemies can be ended without violence. Give a Hollow a memory. Lay an Echo to rest by re-living its memory correctly in a Recital, which returns a fragment of what you burned.
- **Fleeing** into the off-lane Shroud is always allowed: the dark hides those who do not mind being a little forgotten. Hiding raises Silt.
